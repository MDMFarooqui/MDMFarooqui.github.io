Research Scope & Local Context

Throughout September, research focused on analyzing the local digital divide across Manitoba using reports from CBC, Maclean's, and Canadian telecommunications research papers on digital equity. A core finding is that digital poverty is rarely just about internet access—it is heavily tied to device availability. Many low-income Winnipeg households rely solely on smartphones or a single shared screen for multiple students.

Key Findings & Hardware Lifespans

  • Corporate Turn-Over: Local businesses and law firms routinely decommission working laptops and desktops every 3 to 5 years due to software vendor requirements or corporate lease expirations.
  • The Windows 11 Barrier: A significant portion of 2012–2018 era hardware cannot officially run modern proprietary operating systems despite having capable multi-core CPUs and 8GB+ RAM.
  • Open-Source Solution: Deploying lightweight Linux distributions eliminates operating system licensing costs, significantly lowers hardware requirements, and provides a modern, secure browser environment (Firefox/Chromium) suitable for Google Classroom, Microsoft 365 web applications, and research.

Sanitization & Donated Drive Management

To address corporate donor concerns around data privacy, we established a multi-pass overwrite workflow using open-source utilities (shred and DBAN) aligning with NIST-style sanitization concepts. We also adopted a policy to accept hardware without storage drives, sourcing replacement SSDs to ensure zero liability for donor firms.

Initiating formal contact with Winnipeg MSPs, law firms, and e-waste organizations to secure initial hardware donations and establish testing workflows.
