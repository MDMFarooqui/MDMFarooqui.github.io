Project Journal & Research Log

Keeping track of what I learn, what I work on, and what I plan to do as the project develops.

Completed work is documented as it happens. Future entries are listed as goals and may change as the project develops.

What I Worked On

  • Looked through reports and articles from CBC, Maclean's, and other Canadian sources about access to computers and the internet in Manitoba.
  • Researched how a lack of reliable access to computers and internet can affect students and their ability to keep up with schoolwork.
  • Looked into the idea of collecting older computers that are no longer being used but could still be useful after being cleaned, repaired, or upgraded.
  • Started researching lightweight open-source operating systems that could help older computers stay useful without requiring expensive software.
  • Started looking into safe ways to handle data on donated computers before they are given to someone else.

Sources

First 3 weeks completed. The project has begun collecting devices, developing data-sanitization procedures, and selecting Linux distributions. Progress has been limited by exams and collection challenges, but discussions about school credit and potential device donations are underway. Goals may change depending on donations, responses, and available time.

Week 1 — Site & Outreach Completed

  • Launched the project website and organized the project documentation.
  • Started collecting a few donated devices from individuals interested in supporting the project.
  • Reached out to several Winnipeg businesses and organizations that may have unused computers or other equipment.
  • Received a response from the Electronic Recycling Association and spoke with them about the project.
  • Got advice from a few people about collecting equipment and starting the project.

Week 2 — Collection & Process Development Completed

  • Held the project's first collection day on October 10. The number of devices collected was lower than hoped, highlighting the challenges of sourcing donated equipment.
  • Began studying NIST SP 800-88 to understand established data-sanitization practices and started developing DEWY's own data-erasure procedure.
  • Prepared a laptop intake checklist and donor record to document donated equipment, its condition, and authorization for data sanitization.
  • Made a shopping list of tools and equipment needed to inspect, clean, and refurbish older laptops.
  • Spoke with an IT professional about the difficulties businesses face when donating retired equipment due to organizational policies and data-security requirements.
  • Discussed with a teacher whether the project could qualify for school credit as a student-initiated project. A follow-up meeting is scheduled for October 16.
  • Selected Linux Mint editions for refurbished computers based on their hardware capabilities: Cinnamon for capable computers, MATE for lighter computers, and Xfce for older or especially low-resource computers.

Week 3 — Project Development & School Support Completed

  • Made limited progress on hardware collection and refurbishment due to school exams.
  • Met with the dean of my school to discuss whether Digital Equity for Winnipeg Youth could qualify for credit as a student-initiated project.
  • The dean is exploring how to make the project eligible for student-initiated project credit and will also look into whether the school's Technology Resources department may be able to provide devices.

Week 4 — Preparing for Distribution

  • Work on guidelines for deciding when a refurbished computer is ready to be given to a student or family.
  • Create simple instructions to help recipients get started with the installed software.
  • Review the work completed during October and compare it with the project's longer-term goals.
  • Use what was learned during the month to plan the next stage of the project.
